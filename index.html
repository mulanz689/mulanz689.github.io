Research interests

Questions I care about

I am broadly interested in making AI behavior scientifically measurable: isolating where failures arise, testing competing explanations, and identifying when those failures can be anticipated or prevented.

01

Model behavior & structured generalization

When models receive new rules or exceptions, what determines whether they correctly bind those changes to the relevant parts of a structured task?

02

Reliable AI agents & constraint preservation

How do scope rules, permissions, and human intent propagate through an agent stack, and what happens when the information needed for enforcement disappears before an action is taken?

03

Measurement & mechanistic understanding

Can controlled behavioral and representation-level measurements distinguish competing explanations for model failure rather than merely correlate with final accuracy?

04

Prediction & pre-action verification

When stable predictive structure exists, can compact model-specific measurements anticipate implementation failures before generation or execution?

Selected research

A connected research trajectory

I approach AI reliability at several levels—from what information a model represents internally, to whether a constraint remains available inside an agent system, to whether those measurements can distinguish and anticipate failure modes.

Mechanistic interpretability

Selective Rule Updating & Mechanistic Evaluation

Preregistered experiments on Qwen2.5-Instruct (1.5B and 14B) asking whether a model's handling of selectively updated rules can be traced to an identifiable internal mechanism. Residual-stream probes show that rule applicability is highly decodable from hidden states; controlled activation interventions, however, changed an internal readout without changing the model's final answers. The work therefore reports decodability and stops short of claiming a dominant causal mechanism.

mechanistic interpretability · selective rule updating · residual-stream probes · activation interventions · decodability vs. causal control

AI agents · finance

Intent Drift Detection in Financial AI Agents

Studies ambiguity and drift in financial agent workflows and develops an independent multi-engine architecture for evaluating whether agent execution remains consistent with an authorized intent specification.

agent reliability · financial AI · intent drift · runtime evaluation

Agent systems

Constraint Availability and Enforceability

Investigates a systems-level failure mode in which a policy constraint may exist upstream yet fail to reach any component capable of enforcing it. This work separates constraint availability from downstream model compliance.

agent architecture · constraint propagation · system reliability · auditability

Research approach

I care about experiments that can be wrong.

I am especially drawn to problems where a surprising model behavior can be turned into a controlled experiment: isolate the relevant variable, construct competing explanations, and design a test that can falsify the preferred one.

Building real systems has also made me interested in failures that arise between components—not only inside a model—and in preserving the distinction between capability, information availability, and execution.
